# Lighthouse layer

## Core question

Did federal support for lighthouses, beacons, buoys, and piers reduce maritime transaction costs and integrate ports into a national commercial system?

The answer requires implementation evidence and outcomes. The 1789 statute alone establishes authority and intended support, not completed infrastructure or safer navigation.

## Evidence sequence

1. State ownership and jurisdiction before federal transfer.
2. Deed, cession, or site acquisition.
3. Federal authorization and appropriation.
4. Design, contract, construction, and procurement.
5. Keeper appointment, lighting, supply, repair, and inspection.
6. Operational continuity and outages.
7. Voyage use, wrecks, insurance, port traffic, and merchant response.

## First controlled test

Build site histories for a small founding-era port sample, beginning with Boston, New York, Philadelphia/Delaware Bay, Chesapeake Bay, and Charleston. Compare authorization-to-operation delays and operating records with port entries, insurance evidence, and reported wrecks.

## Constraints

- Construction dates, first-light dates, and statutory dates are not interchangeable.
- Port growth can cause lighthouse investment; correlation does not establish infrastructure-led growth.
- Surviving correspondence overrepresents failures, disputes, and unusual expenditures.
- Wreck counts depend on traffic volume and reporting quality.
- Federalization may represent administrative consolidation without an immediate improvement in service.
