# RG 36 customs-district survival frame

## Decision

Do not select ports for transcription yet.

The public NARA descriptions are too uneven to support a defensible sample. Philadelphia has repository-level RG 36 coverage beginning in 1789 and is described as containing ledgers, forms, manifests, cargo records, and collections of duties and fees. Comparable early financial series are not publicly established for New York or Charleston. Boston's publicly visible 1789–1819 collector material concerns lighthouse administration, not customs cash.

A convenience sample would therefore select ports because they are described, not because they are analytically representative.

## Current screen

The initial screen covers twelve districts central to the early customs system or to the ASP missing-return problem. It records only what the public NARA pages establish. A collection-level date does not establish that a specific financial ledger survives for every year within the range.

See `sources/rg36-core-port-survival-screen.csv`.

## Required survival fields

Before a district becomes sample-eligible, resolve:

- exact creating office and collection district;
- NARA series title and identifier;
- inclusive dates and internal gaps;
- volume, box, or entry number;
- record type: impost book, cashbook, bond register, drawback register, manifest, entrance, clearance, abstract, correspondence, or other;
- accounting fields and whether dollar amounts appear;
- merchant, vessel, commodity, and voyage identifiers;
- geographic and temporal aggregation;
- physical location;
- digitization, microfilm, or reproduction status.

## Sampling rule

The sample frame must be constructed before the sample is chosen. At minimum, stratify by:

1. customs scale or ASP-reported duty volume;
2. region;
3. port function and commodity mix;
4. survival quality;
5. complete versus missing ASP returns.

Survival quality can inform feasibility but cannot silently become the substantive selection criterion.

## Priority contrasts

- **Boston:** best M178 correspondence pair, but early RG 36 financial series unconfirmed.
- **Charleston:** critical missing 1790 return, but neither M178 nor public RG 36 descriptions solve it.
- **Philadelphia:** strongest publicly described early RG 36 candidate; high risk of convenience-selection bias.
- **New York:** essential major port with unresolved early RG 36 survival.
- **Smaller ports:** required to test whether famous-port mechanisms generalize.

## Required finding aids

1. NC 154, *Preliminary Inventory of the Bureau of Customs* (1968).
2. National Archives at Philadelphia draft RG 36 inventory.
3. National Archives at Boston district series lists.
4. National Archives at Atlanta RG 36 district series lists.
5. Any New York or Mid-Atlantic regional transfer and custody crosswalks.

## Stop rule

Do not digitize Philadelphia merely because it is the first confirmed collection. First resolve New York, Charleston, Boston, and at least two smaller-port candidates. Otherwise the physical commodity and merchant layers will inherit archival survival bias.
