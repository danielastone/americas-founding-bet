# European-market financing of federal debt

## Core question

How did access to Amsterdam, Antwerp, London-linked exchange markets, and European banking networks affect the cost, credibility, liquidity, and operational flexibility of United States federal debt?

European confidence is not an observable variable. Replace it with contract terms, subscription outcomes, net proceeds, market prices, remittance performance, refinancing, and named intermediaries.

## Initial financing network

- United States Treasury and President;
- Treasurer, Register, and Commissioners of Loans;
- Wilhelm and Jan Willink;
- Nicolaas and Jacob van Staphorst;
- Hubbard and associated Amsterdam houses;
- G. de Wolf at Antwerp;
- European subscribers and secondary holders;
- American merchants and banks providing bills or remittance channels;
- French, Spanish, and other government creditors receiving payment.

Names and firm structures must be authority-controlled. Do not merge bankers, partnerships, and later successor firms merely because surnames recur.

## Required distinctions

- authorized, contracted, subscribed, received, and usable principal;
- coupon rate, issue yield, borrower cost, and after-charge effective cost;
- loan proceeds transferred to the United States versus applied in Europe;
- new borrowing versus rollover or reloan of maturing principal;
- debt-service remittance versus fresh financing;
- nominal exchange conversion versus actual exchange proceeds;
- primary issuance versus secondary-market price.

## First controlled reconstruction

Reconstruct every Amsterdam and Antwerp contract from 1790 through 1794, including the 1793 reloan. Reconcile contract principal to receipts, charges, Treasury transfers, direct European applications, and year-end balances. Then connect changes in terms to United States payment performance and European shocks.

## Hypotheses

1. Regular federal interest service improved placement terms across successive Dutch loans.
2. European financing allowed Treasury to refinance inherited foreign obligations and time remittances without draining domestic specie immediately.
3. Amsterdam intermediation linked customs-backed federal credit to a deeper investor base.
4. War and political disruption in Europe constrained access independently of United States fiscal performance.
5. Foreign proceeds applied directly in Europe provided liquidity but make domestic cash-receipt measures understate borrowing activity.

## Identification limits

- Falling coupons can be offset by higher charges, discounts, shorter maturities, or exchange losses.
- Loan terms reflect European rates and war risk as well as United States credit.
- A signed contract does not establish complete placement or receipt.
- Treasury conversions such as forty cents per guilder are accounting conventions unless reconciled to actual exchange transactions.
- Dutch financing and London credit were connected but not the same market.
- Foreign borrowing capacity depended ultimately on trade-derived revenue and therefore on customs administration, Atlantic commerce, and slavery-linked commodity chains.
