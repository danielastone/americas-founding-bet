# Diplomatic layer: Pinckney’s Treaty

## Core question

Did the Treaty of San Lorenzo convert diplomatic leverage into usable commercial and territorial capacity, and for whom?

The treaty must be analyzed as more than a diplomatic success. Its provisions affected Mississippi navigation, deposit at New Orleans, boundary administration, merchant access, western settlement, Spanish strategy, and Native nations whose territorial position was weakened without their consent.

## Analytical sequence

1. **Negotiation:** instructions, bargaining positions, European war context, and Spanish calculations.
2. **Legal settlement:** article-level rights and obligations in the signed treaty.
3. **Ratification and proclamation:** when obligations became operative.
4. **Implementation:** surveys, military withdrawal, navigation, deposit, customs practice, and disputes.
5. **Distribution:** gains and losses for western settlers, merchants, Spain, Indigenous nations, and enslaved people.

## First test

Create an article-by-article crosswalk, then test Articles IV and XXII against voyage and merchant evidence for Mississippi navigation and New Orleans deposit. Separately trace boundary implementation and Spanish withdrawal alongside evidence from affected Native nations.

## Constraints

- Treaty rights do not prove immediate or uniform commercial use.
- New Orleans access and boundary settlement are related but distinct outcomes.
- Increased western settlement is not an unqualified national gain; it redistributed power and intensified dispossession.
- United States and Spanish diplomatic papers are both required for bargaining claims.
- Merchant and voyage evidence is required before claiming measurable trade effects.
