# NARA T964 source assessment

## Identification

**Microfilm publication:** T964  
**Title:** *Day Book of the Register's Office of the Treasury, 1789–1791*  
**Extent:** one roll  
**Record group:** RG 217, Office of the Register of the Treasury, Receipts and Expenditures Division  
**Official guide:** https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/217.html

The title is corroborated by NARA's published microfilm listing and a 2008 NARA research newsletter. Current public catalog search does not expose a usable T964 item record or digital images. The RG 217 guide places the underlying daybooks, ledgers, journals, and abstracts in the Receipts and Expenditures Division.

## Evidentiary value

T964 may provide transaction-order or daily posting evidence for the opening federal fiscal system. It could resolve:

- when collector remittances entered the Register's books;
- the account names used for customs, tonnage, loans, balances, and expenditures;
- whether the books distinguish receipt by a collector from receipt into Treasury;
- the transition from Confederation accounts to the constitutional Treasury;
- posting lags relative to the ASP collector-return tables.

This is potentially stronger than a retrospective annual total, but only if the daybook identifies the counterparty and account state.

## Risks

1. A daybook is a book of original entry, not automatically a cash book.
2. Posting dates may differ from collection, deposit, settlement, or reporting dates.
3. T964 may reproduce only a selected segment of a larger series.
4. T-series publications may lack a descriptive pamphlet or complete provenance.
5. The reel may begin with inherited Confederation accounts.
6. LOC holdings of a service copy were not confirmed through public web search.
7. The reel may overlap rather than independently confirm LOC shelf 24,495.

## Comparison with LOC shelf 24,495

The two films overlap but answer different questions:

- T964 promises a Register's Office daybook for 1789–1791.
- LOC shelf 24,495 promises tabular extracts of receipts and expenditures for 1784–1790.

Do not call them independent evidence until provenance is known. The LOC extracts may have been compiled from the Register daybook or related central books.
