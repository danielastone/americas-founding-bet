# Sugar-trade layer

## Analytical claim under examination

Sugar-linked commerce was not peripheral cargo. It connected coerced plantation production, Atlantic shipping, merchant finance, refining and distilling, household consumption, and federal customs revenue. The project must measure those links rather than reducing the subject to import quantities.

## Commodity chain

| Stage | Principal evidence | Core measures |
|---|---|---|
| Production | Plantation and colonial records; SlaveVoyages context | Origin, labor regime, output, disruption |
| Maritime movement | Manifests, entrance and clearance registers, voyage records | Vessel, port pair, dates, tonnage, cargo quantity |
| Merchant intermediation | Letters, ledgers, invoices, insurance, credit | Counterparty, price, currency, credit terms, risk, seizure |
| Processing and consumption | Refinery, distillery, wholesale and retail records | Raw/refined form, molasses, rum, conversion, price |
| Federal extraction | Tariff statutes, customs accounts, Treasury reports | Statutory rate, assessed duty, collected duty, drawbacks, enforcement cost |

## Commodity vocabulary

Keep original terms and normalize separately. At minimum distinguish:

- raw, brown, muscovado, clayed, loaf, lump, and refined sugar;
- molasses and syrup;
- rum and other distilled products;
- weight, volume, package, and quality grades;
- origin colony or island;
- re-exported versus entered for domestic consumption.

Do not aggregate sugar, molasses, and rum into one quantity. They use different units, rates, processing stages, and commercial margins.

## First controlled test

Use 1793–1795 as a disruption window. Link the Backus correspondence on British seizures and the 1794 embargo to voyage records, customs/Treasury totals, statutory sugar and molasses rates, and identifiable merchant accounts. Add the Dutilh & Wachsmuth Haiti–Philadelphia–Baltimore evidence as a second network.

## Required cautions

- Surviving merchant papers are not a representative sample.
- Customs values capture declared and recorded trade, not successful smuggling.
- A tariff rate is not the same as an effective rate or collected revenue.
- Origin labels may identify the shipping port rather than the plantation source.
- Sugar profitability cannot be analyzed honestly without plantation slavery, coerced labor, mortality, and political violence.
- The Haitian Revolution is not an external nuisance variable; it changed production, prices, routes, credit risk, and the politics of slavery.
