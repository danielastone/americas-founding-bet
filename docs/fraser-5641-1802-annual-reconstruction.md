# FRASER 5641: 1802 annual P1 reconstruction

## Decision

The December 1802 report supplies more realized annual evidence than the 1801 report, but it still does not support a complete primary `C_s`, `C_u`, or `D_c`.

## Verified annual receipts

For the year ending September 30, 1802:

| Component | Amount | Treatment |
|---|---:|---|
| Merchandise and tonnage duties received into Treasury | $12,280,000 | rounded annual Treasury cash |
| Postage | $50,500 | annual receipt |
| Public-land specie paid into Treasury | $179,575.52 | annual Treasury cash |
| Public-debt evidence accepted for land | $17,162.50 | noncash; exclude from cash denominator |
| Public-land receipts retained by receivers | $129,314.06 | outside Treasury; exclude |

The customs amount is stated only to the nearest ten thousand dollars. It remains useful as a rounded annual component, not an exact reconciliation control.

## Missing denominator components

A complete denominator remains unavailable:

- fees, fines, and penalties are acknowledged but omitted as supposedly immaterial;
- collections of direct-tax arrears during the fiscal year are not stated;
- collections of outstanding internal duties during the fiscal year are not stated;
- incidental receipts and repayments are not totaled;
- the report does not provide an all-receipts control total;
- absence of loan proceeds from the narrative cannot be treated as proof of zero.

The $1,287,600 sale of the remaining Bank shares is a realized asset transaction applied directly to equal bank debt. It belongs in a usable-inflow sensitivity if the transaction path is admitted, not in the strict non-loan denominator.

Using only customs, postage, and land specie would omit acknowledged positive receipts and bias `C_s` upward.

## Debt-service discrepancy

The report says $8,334,757.89 was paid out of Treasury during the year for public-debt principal and interest, excluding the separate $1,287,600 bank-stock transaction.

The visible gross components are:

| Component | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Domestic principal and interest | $4,628,105.39 |
| Foreign officers and registered debt | $9,603.18 |
| Domestic-loan principal | $202,400.00 |
| Public-debt evidence accepted for lands | $17,162.50 |
| Foreign principal and interest | $3,310,874.32 |
| **Visible-component total** | **$8,168,145.39** |
| **Printed total** | **$8,334,757.89** |
| **Unexplained difference** | **$166,612.50** |

The difference is far above the precommitted within-statement tolerance. It is not an OCR error: both page images confirm the printed amounts.

The public-debt evidence accepted for land is also noncash. Therefore neither the printed total nor the visible-component total can enter primary `D_c` without explaining the $166,612.50 and separating cash from noncash principal service.

## What the report does establish

The report directly supports:

- very high customs Treasury receipts in the year ending September 30, 1802;
- exact postage and land-specie components;
- a material asset-sale/debt-retirement transaction;
- large realized debt service;
- a material internal arithmetic discrepancy in the published narrative.

It does not supply a complete annual cash denominator.

## Next source target

The required source is the settled annual Register/Treasurer receipts-and-expenditures account for the year ending September 30, 1802. It must resolve:

1. all noncustoms and financing receipts;
2. the path of the Bank-stock sale proceeds;
3. the $166,612.50 debt-service discrepancy;
4. cash versus public-debt-evidence payments.

Until then, 1802 remains a partial annual reconstruction and does not count toward the coverage floor.
