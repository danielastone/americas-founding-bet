# Falsification priorities

## Purpose

The project should spend its first effort on evidence capable of disproving or materially narrowing the governing thesis. Confirmatory examples, additional thematic layers, and publication work remain secondary.

## Thesis components

The thesis contains claims of unequal importance. They should not be defended as one indivisible package.

| Component | Role | What failure means |
|---|---|---|
| T1. Customs dominance | Essential | If realized customs cash was not the dominant usable federal revenue, the governing thesis fails |
| T2. Fiscal reliability | Essential but revisable | If customs timing and volatility did not support debt service without recurrent emergency finance, replace “dependable capacity” with a weaker contingent-liquidity claim |
| T3. Public-credit transmission | Essential to the finance thesis | If debt performance did not affect financing terms or marketability after common market shocks, drop the transmission claim |
| T4. Louisiana financing dependence | Essential to the endpoint | If the Purchase financing did not rely materially on capacity formed before 1803, Louisiana becomes an adjacent event rather than the thesis endpoint |
| T5. Merchant and Bank mechanisms | Explanatory, not essential | If operational evidence fails, retain only the aggregate fiscal result and remove these mechanisms |
| T6. Transaction-level commodity linkage | Essential to the physical-finance synthesis | If cargo cannot be linked defensibly to customs obligations and cash, present separate fiscal and trade histories rather than one transaction chain |
| T7. Distributional consequences | Supporting | Failure limits inequality claims but does not defeat the core fiscal thesis |

## Ordered tests

### P1. Customs-dominance test

**Question:** After reconciling accounting bases, was realized customs cash the dominant usable federal revenue during 1789–1803?

**Minimum data**

- customs cash received by period;
- internal revenue, direct taxes, land revenue, loans, and other receipts;
- transfers, refunds, drawbacks, collection costs, and unsettled balances;
- debt service and other major cash requirements.

**Falsification conditions**

- customs is not dominant after consistent gross/net and cash/assessment treatment;
- dependence appears only because loans or other receipts are misclassified;
- reported customs totals cannot be reconciled within a disclosed tolerance;
- cash arrived too late to meet the obligations attributed to it.

**Decision**

- **Fail:** abandon the governing thesis.
- **Mixed:** narrow to specified years, ports, or accounting states.
- **Pass:** proceed to liquidity and endpoint tests.

This is the first priority because it is both decisive and cheaper than merchant reconstruction.

### P2. Louisiana financing-independence test

**Question:** Did the 1803 financing materially use fiscal and credit capacity established before the Purchase, or could it be explained as an isolated diplomatic-financial transaction?

**Minimum data**

- authorization and obligations;
- claims convention;
- stock issued and delivered;
- market value and net proceeds;
- Baring and Hope contracts, commissions, exchange, timing, and settlement;
- existing debt-service schedule and Treasury liquidity;
- feasible contemporary financing alternatives.

**Falsification conditions**

- prior customs-backed debt performance is not connected to the terms or placement;
- purchase obligations required a break from, rather than use of, existing capacity;
- existing debt service was interrupted or materially destabilized;
- intermediaries relied primarily on factors unrelated to United States fiscal performance;
- the financing cannot be reconciled from face value to settlement value.

**Decision**

- **Fail:** remove Louisiana as proof or stress test; treat it as a separate case.
- **Mixed:** retain it only as an application of some existing instruments.
- **Pass:** retain the endpoint.

This test comes before broad port and merchant work because the endpoint is doing major argumentative work.

### P3. Timing and liquidity test

**Question:** Did customs cash arrive with sufficient regularity to support scheduled debt service, or did borrowing and payment management bridge chronic mismatches?

**Minimum data**

- monthly or quarterly collections where available;
- bond issue and maturity schedules;
- remittance dates;
- Treasury balances and transfers;
- interest and principal due dates;
- short-term borrowing or delayed payments.

**Falsification conditions**

- annual totals conceal recurrent inability to meet scheduled obligations;
- debt service depended primarily on refinancing rather than realized revenue;
- material customs arrears or defaults were systematically excluded;
- the First Bank or merchant credit substituted for weak fiscal realization rather than transmitting strength.

**Decision**

- **Fail:** replace “dependable” with “fragile, bridged, or episodic.”
- **Pass:** the reliability language survives.

### P4. Public-credit market test

**Question:** Did federal fiscal performance improve the marketability or financing terms of United States obligations beyond movements attributable to European markets and war?

**Minimum data**

- security transaction prices or bid/ask quotations;
- yields or implied yields;
- exchange rates and transfer costs;
- comparable sovereign securities;
- dated fiscal news and payment events;
- European war and credit controls.

**Falsification conditions**

- United States security movements are explained by common market factors;
- payment performance has no observable relationship to price, yield, placement, or commissions;
- marketability is asserted from successful issuance without measuring terms;
- only retrospective praise supports the claim.

**Decision**

- **Fail:** retain debt payment as an accounting fact but drop the marketability mechanism.
- **Pass:** proceed to specific intermediaries.

### P5. Adverse-case transaction test

**Question:** Can the proposed cargo–customs–credit mechanism explain both successful and failed transactions?

**Minimum design**

Select at least:

- one completed import or re-export chain;
- one defaulted, delayed, seized, denied-drawback, or otherwise impaired chain;
- a documented selection rule established before outcomes are examined.

**Falsification conditions**

- the links cannot be established from independent records;
- customs credit or drawback timing is immaterial to working capital;
- the successful case depends on exceptional access unavailable to the comparison case;
- only surviving successful merchants can be observed.

**Decision**

- **Fail:** remove the transaction-level mechanism.
- **Pass:** expand cautiously to a bounded sample.

The first merchant case should not be selected because its records are unusually complete and successful.

### P6. Port and commodity generalization test

**Question:** Does the mechanism survive outside the first transaction and port?

This is deliberately late. Port panels and commodity expansion are expensive and cannot rescue a thesis already defeated by P1–P5.

**Falsification conditions**

- results depend on one dominant port, commodity, merchant network, or exceptional shock;
- port exports cannot be connected to production origin;
- re-exports are misclassified as domestic production;
- missing districts or changing classifications drive the result.

## Precommitted stopping rules

- Do not begin broad state, port, commodity, welfare, infrastructure, or global-market extraction before P1 and P2 are resolved.
- Do not use merchant cases to compensate for a failed federal accounting test.
- Do not use aggregate federal totals to claim a transaction mechanism that fails P5.
- Do not add causal language after a descriptive test fails.
- Preserve failed tests and contradictory evidence in the repository.
- Reframe or terminate the thesis when a stated essential component fails; do not quietly redefine success after observing results.

## Immediate execution order

1. Specify and populate the minimum federal receipt-and-debt-service table needed for P1.
2. Reconcile the Louisiana financing for P2 in parallel accounting terms, but do not infer dependence yet.
3. Increase temporal frequency only as required for P3.
4. Build the security-market comparison for P4.
5. Select one successful and one adverse merchant transaction for P5.
6. Expand ports and commodities only after the earlier tests survive.

## Publication rule

A negative or mixed result is a valid project outcome. The repository should report whether the evidence supports:

- the full customs-to-credit-to-Purchase thesis;
- a narrower customs-to-debt-service thesis;
- a fragile-liquidity interpretation;
- separate fiscal and commodity histories;
- or rejection of the proposed unifying mechanism.

The research question is more valuable than preserving the original answer.
