# State-level archival plan, 1789–1803

## Finding

No uniform state archive exists for this project. State fiscal systems used different offices, accounts, tax bases, calendars, and retention practices. A balanced state panel assembled from headline totals would create false comparability.

The workable design has three layers:

1. A federal cross-state spine from state loan-office records.
2. State treasury, comptroller, auditor, and tax records.
3. Commodity inspection, production-proxy, transport, and market records linked to port hinterlands.

## Geographic scope

| State | Project role | Associated node |
|---|---|---|
| New York | Northern commercial and banking state | New York City |
| Pennsylvania | Federal financial center and grain/flour hinterland | Philadelphia |
| Maryland | Tobacco-to-flour transition and port competition | Baltimore |
| South Carolina | Rice, indigo, cotton, slavery, and Atlantic finance | Charleston |
| Kentucky | Western production and Mississippi-access demand | Ohio–Mississippi corridor |
| Tennessee | Western state formation and taxable-property comparison | Cumberland–Mississippi corridor |

Virginia is a likely comparison because of tobacco, debt, and western claims, but adding it now would widen the project before the six-state archive can be assessed.

## Cross-state archival spine

### National Archives, Record Group 53

**AFB-S066** is the first source to use. Section 53.3 inventories state loan-office records created in implementing public borrowing and the loan of 1790.

Useful series include:

- Accounts for the loan of 1790
- Accounts current
- Registers of certificates
- Journals and ledgers for funded securities
- Interest and dividend abstracts
- Pennsylvania commissioners-of-loans letter books, 1793–1804
- State-specific microfilm publications

This source supports state-level implementation of federal debt funding. It does not measure a state's own treasury, taxes, banks, or commodity economy.

### Required crosswalk

For each state-year, preserve:

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| state | Historical jurisdiction |
| office | Federal state loan office or state fiscal office |
| fiscal_year_basis | Calendar, statutory, or office reporting period |
| original_account | Exact account title |
| instrument | Certificate, funded stock, cash, tax receivable, warrant, or other |
| face_value | Legal principal |
| market_value | Observed value, if separately available |
| cash_flow | Actual receipt or payment |
| interest | Accrued and paid separately |
| holder | Registered holder where recorded |
| federal_state_flag | Federal obligation, state obligation, or disputed |
| assumption_status | Eligible, subscribed, rejected, settled, or unknown |
| source_id | Registered source |
| archive_reference | Record group, series, volume, folio, image, or item |

## State archive assessment

### Pennsylvania — first priority

Sources **AFB-S067–S068** provide the strongest immediate archive.

Target series:

- Comptroller General journals, ledgers, and settlement accounts
- Tax and county-officer accounts
- Abstracts and certificates of public debt
- General Loan Office records
- State Treasurer appropriation and balance books
- Federal–state settlement accounts

Why Pennsylvania first:

- The fiscal series overlap the full project period.
- Philadelphia connects state finance, the First Bank, federal Treasury operations, merchants, customs, and flour exports.
- The state archive supplies both transaction-level and summarized accounts.

Risk: many volumes contain earlier transactions compiled in the 1790s. Compilation date and transaction date must remain separate.

### Maryland — second priority

**AFB-S069** provides item-level access to state papers, auditors, treasurers, property, commodity payments, and public works. Maryland State Archives listings also expose tobacco and flour inspection references, but an exact continuous 1789–1803 series has not yet been established.

Target request:

- Treasurer of the Western Shore journals, ledgers, receipts, and disbursements
- Auditor General accounts
- State debt and federal-assumption records
- Tobacco and flour inspection returns by warehouse or district
- Baltimore roads, harbor, lighthouse, and navigation records
- Bank charter filings and periodic financial returns

Risk: the searchable State Papers are heterogeneous correspondence, not an accounting series.

### South Carolina — second priority

**AFB-S070** identifies early Treasurer tax duplicate books. These may provide property and tax-base measures connected to Charleston's commodity hinterland.

Target request:

- Treasurer journals, ledgers, tax duplicates, and receipt books, 1789–1803
- State debt and assumption settlement records
- Legislative papers on banks, taxes, rice, indigo, tobacco, and cotton
- Commodity inspection or export-quality records
- Charleston harbor and navigation expenditure records
- Bank of South Carolina or other early bank charter and return records, where held

Risk: taxable property should not be converted into production or income. Enslaved people may appear as taxable property; the project must preserve the coercive legal category while never treating persons as commodities in the data model.

### Tennessee — targeted western comparison

**AFB-S071** confirms county tax lists from 1796, but survival is uneven.

Target series:

- County tax lists, 1796–1803
- Comptroller journals, warrants, and settlements
- State debt and federal-account correspondence
- River, road, warehouse, inspection, and navigation legislation or returns
- Merchant and court records documenting produce claims, debts, and river shipment

Risk: rapid county formation and changing boundaries make nominal county comparisons misleading.

### Kentucky — archivist query required

**AFB-S072** is only a portal. It does not yet identify usable early fiscal series.

Send a bounded query requesting:

- Auditor of Public Accounts or Treasurer journals, ledgers, warrants, and annual statements, 1792–1803
- State and county tax books with surviving counties and date coverage
- Bank of Kentucky or predecessor banking records only within the project dates
- Warehouse and inspection records for tobacco, flour, hemp, or other goods
- Ohio and Mississippi river, road, ferry, and port records
- Court or merchant series with debt, bills, produce, and shipment evidence

Until exact series are confirmed, Kentucky is a research gap, not a completed archive.

### New York — archivist query required

The online search did not establish a sufficiently precise, continuous 1789–1803 fiscal series. Do not substitute the existence of the New York State Archives for source coverage.

Request:

- Comptroller journals, ledgers, vouchers, tax accounts, and annual statements, 1789–1803
- State debt and federal assumption records
- Bank charter, incorporation, examination, or return records
- Turnpike, river, harbor, and navigation finance records before 1804
- Commodity inspection records tied to New York City exports
- Clear series identifiers, date spans, gaps, access restrictions, and digitization status

New York is commercially central but may be expensive to reconstruct. Its importance does not cure missing records.

## Commodity archive strategy

State records rarely provide a clean production series. Use a hierarchy of evidence:

1. Inspection returns with commodity, quantity, grade, location, and date.
2. Tax records explicitly tied to commodity acreage, output, warehouse, or processing.
3. Warehouse, road, river, ferry, and market records.
4. Merchant receipts and shipment records linked to state origins.
5. Port exports only after origin is independently established.
6. Census or later retrospective estimates as validation, not transaction evidence.

Never assign a port export to the port's state solely because it cleared there.

## Bank archive strategy

For each state, locate:

- Incorporation act and charter amendments
- Subscription and paid-in capital records
- Director and officer lists
- Balance statements or statutory returns
- Discount, deposit, and note ledgers
- Examination or legislative investigation records
- Correspondence with the state treasury or First Bank
- Failure, protest, litigation, and liquidation records

A charter proves authorization. It does not prove capitalization, lending volume, note circulation, solvency, or state support.

## First extraction

Start with Pennsylvania, not all six states.

1. Select RG-4 and RG-8 series covering 1789–1803.
2. Inventory volumes, dates, account bases, digitization, and access.
3. Extract a five-year pilot: 1789, 1791, 1795, 1799, and 1803 where records permit.
4. Reconcile state debt, taxes, receipts, expenditures, and federal settlement entries.
5. Link Philadelphia customs and flour-trade observations.
6. Decide whether the data can support annual comparison before expanding.

This pilot will reveal whether the state layer is analytically viable. Starting six archival projects simultaneously would produce catalogs, not evidence.

## Deliverables

1. State archive series register with exact identifiers.
2. State fiscal accounting crosswalk.
3. Pennsylvania pilot dataset.
4. Six-state archival coverage and missingness matrix.
5. Commodity inspection and production-proxy register.
6. Bank charter-versus-operation register.
7. Decision on whether a multi-state panel is defensible or whether state evidence should remain comparative case studies.
