# NARA M178 source assessment

## Identification

**Microfilm publication:** M178  
**Title:** *Correspondence of the Secretary of the Treasury with Collectors of Customs, 1789–1833*  
**Extent:** 39 rolls  
**Record group:** RG 56, General Records of the Department of the Treasury  
**Descriptive pamphlet:** National Archives and Records Service, 1957, nine pages  
**Official guide:** https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/056.html

## Provenance finding

M178 is not a continuously preserved original correspondence series. NARA descriptions state that the letters were assembled from various sources to replace Treasury records destroyed by the 1833 fire. The microfilm includes copies of letters sent to collectors and copies of letters received.

This materially limits inference:

- absence of a letter is not evidence that no communication or problem existed;
- surviving correspondence may overrepresent disputes, unusual cases, or copied directives;
- the compilation may combine originals, copies, and reconstructed files;
- quantitative counts of letters by port or year are not measures of administrative intensity.

## Relevant content

The correspondence covers:

- collection of customs duties;
- import and export controls;
- vessel documentation and measurement;
- smuggling enforcement;
- revenue cutters;
- lighthouses and marine hospitals;
- navigation and passenger laws;
- appointments and dismissals of customhouse personnel.

For P1, use only documents addressing:

1. collector remittance schedules and deposit instructions;
2. missing or delayed quarterly returns;
3. customs bonds, extensions, defaults, and enforcement;
4. treatment of drawbacks and collection expenses;
5. distinctions among duties accrued, collected, remitted, and received into Treasury;
6. named ports or periods identified as anomalies in central accounts.

## What M178 cannot do

M178 cannot establish the national customs-receipt denominator. It cannot prove completeness, and it should not be used to estimate aggregate remittances from the frequency or monetary amounts mentioned in surviving letters.

## Acquisition rule

Do not acquire all 39 rolls. First obtain the pamphlet's roll-date or roll-content table. Then select only rolls covering 1789–1803 and, within those, prioritize named ports and dates generated by the RG 217 and ASP audits.

## Relationship to M175

M175 contains letters sent by the Secretary of the Treasury to customs collectors beginning in 1789. It may preserve a more systematic outgoing series, but sent instructions still do not prove collector compliance or cash transfer. Compare M178 and M175 only to establish provenance and missing correspondence, not to double-count documents.

## Roll-table result

The 1957 pamphlet arranges rolls primarily by port or district, separating letters **to** collectors from letters **from** collectors. The early-period selection is recorded in `sources/nara-m178-early-roll-selection.csv`.

The coverage is structurally incomplete for the thesis:

- Boston is the strongest two-way early case: rolls 6–7 contain letters to the collector from 1789–1808, and roll 11 contains letters from the collector from 1790–1812.
- New York has no early coverage: roll 17 begins in 1828–1829.
- Charleston has no early coverage: roll 32 begins in 1830–1831.
- Philadelphia has outgoing letters beginning in 1790, but incoming letters begin only in 1803.
- Several smaller ports have two-way coverage, including Alexandria, New Bern, Portsmouth, and Providence.

This distribution confirms that M178 cannot be a representative national sample. Its best use is a predeclared case study of Boston and selected smaller ports, plus evidence of Treasury instructions to major ports where incoming letters are missing.

## Immediate next step

Do not acquire all 39 rolls. For the 1789–1790 missing-return problem, start with Boston rolls 6 and 11. Roll 6 covers Treasury letters to Boston for 1789–1796; roll 11 covers Boston letters to Treasury for 1790–1812. Add roll 7 only when extending beyond 1796. Charleston’s missing 1790 quarter cannot be resolved through M178.
