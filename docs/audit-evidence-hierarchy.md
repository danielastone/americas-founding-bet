# Audit evidence hierarchy

## Purpose

This hierarchy determines what evidence is sufficient for each historical assertion in *America’s Founding Bet*. It prevents legal texts, published summaries, catalog descriptions, and vivid correspondence from being treated as interchangeable proof.

Evidence strength is **assertion-specific**. A statute is conclusive evidence of enacted legal authority but weak evidence of implementation. A merchant ledger may be strong evidence of one transaction but weak evidence of national prevalence. No source receives a universal rank independent of the claim being tested.

## Evidence-quality criteria

Every source–claim link is evaluated on five dimensions:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Does the evidence address the exact assertion, period, entity, and accounting state? |
| Directness | Is the fact observed in the record, reported by another actor, reconstructed, or inferred? |
| Reliability | Who created the record, for what purpose, under what controls or incentives? |
| Independence | Does corroboration come from a genuinely separate record system or merely repeat the same report? |
| Completeness | What universe produced the record, what is missing, and can absence be interpreted? |

A higher-ranked source that does not address the assertion is not better evidence than a lower-ranked source that does.


## Hindsight-bias and ex-ante risk protocol

Historical success does not establish that a decision was prudent when made, and an adverse outcome does not establish that the decision was unreasonable. Every decision claim must reconstruct the information set, feasible alternatives, constraints, and perceived risks at the decision date before examining realized outcomes.

### Information cutoff

Each decision or risk assessment must specify:

- `decision_date` or bounded decision window;
- `information_cutoff`;
- actor or institution whose perspective is being reconstructed;
- evidence demonstrably available to that actor by the cutoff;
- information that existed but is not shown to have been available;
- later evidence admitted only as outcome, validation, or consequence.

Sources created after the cutoff cannot be used to improve the actor’s forecast. A later retrospective statement may document memory or interpretation, but it does not automatically belong to the ex-ante information set.

### Required separation

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ex-ante signal | Information available before the decision |
| Belief or expectation | What an identified actor said, priced, budgeted, or acted as though they expected |
| Exposure | Amount, route, obligation, actor, or institution subject to loss |
| Risk | Uncertain event linked to an exposure and transmission mechanism |
| Mitigation | Diversification, security, insurance, reserve, treaty, enforcement, or contractual response |
| Realized event | What actually occurred |
| Realized effect | Measured consequence after the event |
| Retrospective interpretation | Later explanation, memory, or historical synthesis |

Possibility, perceived probability, exposure, expected loss, realized loss, and retrospective importance are not interchangeable.

### Risk taxonomy

Assess only risks connected to the empirical spine:

| Risk class | Examples for this project |
|---|---|
| Fiscal | Revenue shortfall, expenditure pressure, debt-service insufficiency |
| Liquidity and refinancing | Collection delay, bond maturity mismatch, inability to place or roll obligations |
| Market | Commodity price, security price, interest-rate, and exchange-rate movement |
| Credit and counterparty | Merchant, surety, bank, foreign intermediary, or sovereign nonperformance |
| Operational | Customs administration, remittance, documentation, enforcement, fraud, and port disruption |
| Legal and sovereignty | Treaty interpretation, deposit rights, boundary dispute, authority, title, and effective control |
| Geopolitical | War, seizure, embargo, revolution, foreign exclusion, and regime change |
| Concentration | Dependence on a port, commodity, revenue source, merchant network, or foreign market |
| Information and model | Incomplete returns, delayed news, unreliable estimates, and mistaken causal assumptions |
| Distributional | Risk transferred to regions, merchants, consumers, enslaved people, or Indigenous nations |

### Risk-assessment record

Each material decision or mechanism must record:

- objective and success criterion stated without reference to later outcomes;
- feasible alternatives available at the time, including delay or no action;
- constraints and commitments already in place;
- contemporaneous signals supporting and contradicting the decision;
- exposure, risk event, transmission channel, and potential consequence;
- mitigation and residual risk;
- uncertainty range and basis;
- realized outcome in a separate field;
- difference between expected and realized conditions;
- evidence that would show the decision was reckless, robust, lucky, unlucky, or indeterminate.

Do not assign numerical probabilities unless contemporaneous prices, frequencies, forecasts, or a transparent bounding method support them. Qualitative labels must define their evidentiary basis.

### Counterfactual discipline

The comparison must be a feasible option available to the historical actor, not a modern analyst’s preferred policy. For the Louisiana endpoint, comparisons may include continued treaty dependence, renewed negotiation, military escalation, delayed acquisition, or alternative financing only when contemporaneous evidence shows that actors considered or could practicably execute them.

Later population growth, territorial expansion, agricultural output, or national power cannot be used to justify the 1803 decision. Those are realized consequences shaped by later events and policies. The endpoint assessment instead asks whether the financing structure, access problem, alternatives, and downside exposures were acceptable given information available in 1802–1803.

### Bias controls

- **Outcome bias:** evaluate process and information separately from result.
- **Survivorship bias:** search for failed merchants, defaulted bonds, lost cargoes, excluded routes, and abandoned alternatives.
- **Selection bias:** document why surviving reports, claims, and correspondence entered the record.
- **Teleology:** do not treat later continental expansion as the inevitable purpose of earlier fiscal institutions.
- **Presentism:** use contemporary legal, commercial, accounting, and political categories before applying modern analytical labels.
- **Narrative compression:** preserve decision sequence, information arrival, and reversibility rather than collapsing events into an inevitable chain.

## Default hierarchy

| Level | Evidence class | Examples | Permitted use | Principal limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Underlying contemporaneous transaction or account record | Customs entry, bond, cashbook, Treasury warrant, ledger posting, bank discount, bill of exchange, insurance policy, invoice, cargo manifest | Prove a recorded transaction, balance, date, party, instrument, or accounting state | Survival, authenticity, transcription, entity linkage, and representativeness |
| E2 | Contemporaneous reconciliation or complete administrative return | Customhouse return, Treasury account current, audited statement, district return, official debt-service schedule | Establish bounded totals and reconcile lower-level records | Compilation methods and omitted districts or adjustments may be unclear |
| E3 | Contemporaneous official report, correspondence, or legislative submission | Treasury report, collector letter, diplomatic dispatch, report later printed in the American State Papers | Establish official knowledge, explanation, policy, reported totals, or exceptions | May be selective, argumentative, aggregated, or unreconciled |
| E4 | Retrospective official compilation or faithful published transcription | Printed American State Papers volumes, later documentary editions, retrospective official tables | Locate and reproduce earlier documents; support chronology and reported figures after checking | Editorial selection, delayed publication, transcription error, and loss of archival context |
| E5 | Contemporaneous nonofficial observation | Merchant correspondence, newspapers, price currents, private account summaries, traveler reports | Establish private expectations, quotations, market conditions, or a bounded case | Incentives, hearsay, inconsistent definitions, selection, and limited generalizability |
| E6 | Catalog, finding aid, metadata record, index, or institutional description | LOC catalog record, NARA finding aid, archive guide, collection essay | Discovery, identity, custody, hierarchy, and citation | Does not prove document contents or historical implementation |
| E7 | Scholarly dataset or secondary interpretation | Monograph, article, edited statistical series, modern database | Context, historiography, comparison, methods, and leads | Dependent on the author’s selection, transformations, and cited sources |
| E8 | Unverified derivative or tertiary material | Unsourced web summary, generated text, copied chronology | Discovery only | Uncontrolled provenance and unknown error propagation |

## Assertion-specific rules

### Legal authority

For enactment, legal terms, effective dates, repeal, and territorial language:

1. enrolled act, treaty, or official Statutes at Large text;
2. authenticated official edition or treaty series;
3. contemporary administrative interpretation;
4. later legal commentary.

Legal authority does not prove collection, payment, compliance, effective control, or commercial use.

### Accounting existence and amount

For customs cash, debt service, purchase proceeds, balances, and payments:

1. underlying ledger, account, warrant, receipt, or instrument;
2. administrative reconciliation or complete return;
3. independently prepared official report;
4. retrospective compilation or secondary series.

A statutory appropriation is not evidence that cash was collected or paid. An assessed duty is not cash. A bond is not realized revenue. Face value is not market value or net proceeds.

### Physical trade

For cargo, voyage, quantity, origin, destination, and trade type:

1. linked entry, clearance, manifest, cargo line, invoice, or vessel record;
2. complete customs-district return;
3. national or congressional trade table;
4. merchant narrative or newspaper report.

Port of export does not prove production origin. A vessel clearance does not prove arrival. Cargo value does not prove profit.

### Merchant finance

For credit, bonding, insurance, working capital, and settlement:

1. executed instrument plus payment, maturity, or disposition record;
2. linked ledger postings and correspondence;
3. one-sided ledger or correspondence;
4. general institutional rule or secondary description.

A merchant’s statement of intent does not prove execution. A customs-credit statute does not prove that a named merchant received or repaid credit.

### Market price and security performance

For commodity prices, exchange rates, and security prices:

1. executed transaction or account;
2. dated price current or market quotation from a defined market;
3. independent contemporary quotation;
4. retrospective series with documented construction.

Quoted price, bid, ask, par value, transaction price, and accounting conversion remain separate.

### Diplomatic and sovereignty claims

For treaty access, deposit rights, territorial claims, and effective control:

1. treaty or official instrument for legal terms;
2. diplomatic and administrative records for interpretation and enforcement;
3. shipping, seizure, customs, or route records for commercial use;
4. later narrative synthesis.

A treaty proves agreed language, not compliance. A sovereignty claim does not establish uncontested jurisdiction, Indigenous consent, boundary resolution, or lower transport cost.

## American State Papers treatment

The American State Papers are not one evidentiary class in every use.

- The printed volume is E4: a retrospective official compilation.
- The dated report or letter reproduced within it may preserve E3 evidence.
- A table transcribed from the volume remains E3 or E4 unless reconciled to E1 or E2 records.
- The collection’s index, search result, and catalog metadata are E6.
- Citation must include class, volume, document title or number, original document date, printed page, image or resource identifier, and transcription status.
- Publication date must never replace the underlying report or observation date.
- Tables require visual verification; searchable text is discovery assistance, not verified transcription.
- Absence from the compilation cannot prove that no underlying report or transaction existed.

For the federal fiscal spine, American State Papers totals should be reconciled to Treasury accounts or administrative returns where available. For merchant mechanisms, the Papers can identify rules, disputes, and exceptions but cannot replace linked customhouse and merchant records.

## Audit procedures

Use the procedure that matches the assertion:

| Procedure | Application |
|---|---|
| Vouching | Move from an analytical observation back to the exact source image, page, and record |
| Tracing | Move from a source universe into the dataset to test completeness |
| Recalculation | Recompute duties, interest, discounts, exchange, drawbacks, and totals from original components |
| Reconciliation | Explain differences between ledgers, district returns, Treasury reports, and published compilations |
| Cutoff testing | Verify entry, assessment, maturity, collection, remittance, and reporting dates around period boundaries |
| Classification testing | Test import versus re-export, assessed versus collected, face versus market value, and port versus origin |
| Corroboration | Compare genuinely independent record systems |
| Negative-evidence testing | Document archival coverage before interpreting silence or absence |
| Contradiction search | Seek records that would falsify or materially narrow the claim |

## Corroboration rules

Corroboration is required when:

- the source is retrospective, selected, self-interested, or derivative;
- a table lacks a documented compilation method;
- an inferred entity link materially affects a result;
- a causal or distributional conclusion depends on one archive or one surviving merchant;
- a reported total cannot be reconciled to the underlying accounting state.

Two publications reproducing the same Treasury report are one evidentiary origin, not two independent confirmations.

## Claim gates

### Accounting claim

May be marked **validated** only when:

- the accounting state and period are defined;
- the total is recalculated or reconciled;
- cutoff and classification are tested;
- residual differences are quantified and explained;
- provenance reaches a source image or archival item.

### Mechanism claim

May be marked **analysis_ready** only when:

- linked observations show the proposed sequence;
- timing is consistent with the mechanism;
- an alternative explanation and contradictory evidence are recorded;
- the case is not generalized beyond its sampling frame.

### Causal claim

May be published as causal only when:

- the accounting and mechanism gates are satisfied;
- a defensible counterfactual or comparison is specified;
- treatment timing and exposure are measured;
- common shocks and selection are addressed;
- robustness and falsification tests are reported.

Chronology, plausibility, and correlation do not satisfy this gate.

## Evidence-status crosswalk

| Repository state | Minimum evidence condition |
|---|---|
| discovery_only | E6–E8 lead or unresolved reference |
| item_identified | Specific source object located |
| source_identified | Bounded report, edition, or dataset located |
| transcribed | Relevant content extracted from the source image and checked |
| normalized | Original values preserved and mapped under documented rules |
| validated | Applicable vouching, recalculation, reconciliation, cutoff, and classification tests passed |
| analysis_ready | Evidence satisfies the registered claim’s accounting, mechanism, or causal gate |

Metadata verification never advances evidence beyond `item_identified` or `source_identified` by itself.

## Required claim-evidence record

Every material claim–source relationship must record:

- `claim_id`
- `source_id` and exact item or image locator
- assertion tested
- evidence level
- supporting, contradicting, or qualifying direction
- observed, reported, reconstructed, inferred, or imputed status
- relevant period and population
- information cutoff and actor perspective
- ex-ante, realized-outcome, or retrospective-evidence classification
- exposure, risk, mitigation, feasible alternatives, and residual uncertainty where applicable
- procedure performed
- result and unexplained difference
- independence group
- limitation and selection risk
- reviewer and review date
- publication eligibility

## Failure conditions

A claim fails the evidence audit when:

- it rests only on legal authority for an implementation assertion;
- a published aggregate is treated as a transaction;
- repeated copies of one report are counted as independent corroboration;
- OCR is used without image verification;
- an unexplained reconciliation difference is suppressed;
- missing archival coverage is interpreted as zero;
- a vivid merchant case is generalized without a sampling argument;
- post-1803 evidence is used directly for pre-1804 behavior without a continuity test;
- contradictory evidence is omitted from the claim record;
- later outcomes are used to improve an ex-ante forecast;
- historical success is treated as proof that risk was low or the decision was prudent;
- a modern counterfactual is substituted for alternatives actually feasible at the time.

## Governing principle

The project should make the narrowest claim supported by the strongest relevant evidence. More sources do not compensate for a broken chain of proof.
