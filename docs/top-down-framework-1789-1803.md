# Top-down research framework, 1789–1803

## Governing thesis

Between 1789 and 1803, the United States converted customs cash generated by Atlantic commodity trade into debt service and marketable public obligations. Customhouses, merchant credit, the First Bank, and European intermediaries connected cargo transactions to federal finance. The Louisiana Purchase is the endpoint and stress test of this capacity, not proof that the system caused expansion or created Mississippi commerce.

## Admission rule

A source, dataset, or narrative enters the core only if it:

1. tests a registered claim;
2. supplies an observation to a canonical table;
3. measures a link in the empirical spine;
4. identifies the federal, port, merchant, or narrowly necessary state unit;
5. states what would contradict the claim.

Material failing this test is supporting context, deferred work, or out of scope.

## Empirical spine

Cargo or voyage → merchant finance → customs assessment and cash realization → Treasury revenue and debt service → security performance and marketability → domestic or European intermediation → Louisiana obligation and settlement.

The direction of the historical narrative follows this chain. Feedback effects may be tested, but they do not justify separate thematic projects.

## Claim hierarchy

| Tier | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Accounting | Establish quantities and identities | Customs cash, debt service, cargo quantity, bond payment, security proceeds |
| 2. Mechanism | Reconstruct transmission | Working-capital interval, Treasury transfer, drawback timing, security placement |
| 3. Causal | Estimate effects against a counterfactual | Effect of customs credit, Bank access, treaty change, or prior credit capacity |

Tier 3 claims cannot be inferred from chronology or promoted from aggregate correlation. The canonical claims are in `claims/claim-register.csv`.

## Required analytical levels

### Federal

Reconcile customs cash, other revenue, debt service, Treasury–Bank flows, foreign placement, and Louisiana obligations. Federal accounts form the spine.

### City and port

Measure where cargoes, customs obligations, financial services, and shocks met. Begin with one evidence-supported Atlantic port pair. Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Baltimore, and Charleston are candidates, not a promised balanced panel.

### Merchant

Reconstruct at least one transaction from cargo acquisition through voyage, customs obligation, financing, insurance, sale or re-export, and settlement. Vivid surviving papers do not by themselves make a representative sample.

### State

Use state evidence only to identify a fiscal relationship, commodity origin, banking connection, or hinterland feeding a selected transaction or port. Do not build parallel state histories.

### New Orleans

Treat New Orleans as a foreign-colonial port before transfer and an endpoint case afterward. Do not pool it mechanically with continuously U.S.-administered customs districts.

## Commodity design

Begin with:

- one Caribbean import/re-export chain, preferably sugar or coffee;
- one domestic export chain, preferably flour or tobacco;
- one materiality check for an omitted northern maritime commodity.

Other commodities remain candidates until consistent quantity, unit, origin, destination, price, and financing evidence justifies inclusion.

## Time design

| Period | Role |
|---|---|
| Before 1789 | Baseline only |
| 1789–1791 | Customs, funding, assumption, and Bank design |
| 1792–1796 | Implementation and early shocks |
| 1797–1800 | Credit stress, enforcement, and re-export |
| 1801–1803 | Access crisis and Purchase financing |
| Narrow 1804 tail | Execution of 1803 transfer, claims, or financing only |
| Later | Validation or consequence, excluded from main estimates |

## Minimum viable project

1. Reconciled federal fiscal table, 1789–1803.
2. Tariff and customs-law chronology through 1803.
3. One defensible Atlantic port pair.
4. One end-to-end merchant transaction.
5. Louisiana financing reconciliation.

Commodity corridors and shocks enter only through those outputs. Distributional analysis follows after the transaction and fiscal evidence can support it.

## Priority order

1. Freeze definitions and claims.
2. Build the customs-law chronology.
3. Populate and reconcile the federal fiscal table.
4. Score port coverage and select one pair.
5. Reconstruct one merchant transaction.
6. Reconcile the Louisiana financing.
7. Test registered claims and only then expand.

## Stop conditions

Do not add a layer when it:

- lacks a registered claim, canonical observation, or bounded source path;
- begins after the narrow 1804 execution tail;
- duplicates an existing mechanism under a new label;
- adds narrative breadth without improving measurement or identification;
- requires broad new geographic or thematic coverage before the MVP exists.

Infrastructure, global-market context, diplomatic chronology, welfare, and publication planning remain supporting or deferred unless admitted through this rule.
