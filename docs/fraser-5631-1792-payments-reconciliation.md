# FRASER 5631: 1792 actual-payments reconciliation

## Source and scope

Primary source: *General Statement of the Appropriations made by law, and of the Expenditures of the United States in relation thereto, from the first day of January to the last day of December, 1792*, FRASER item 5631, PDF ordinal pages 42–44.

This transcription admits the row labeled **“Amount of payments during the year 1792.”** It does not substitute appropriations or unexpended balances for payments.

## Image-verification result

The payment row contains 22 statutory-purpose columns. Manual image transcription, followed by OCR cross-checking, produces:

| Control | Dollars |
|---|---:|
| Printed total payments | 8,967,692.05 |
| Sum of 22 transcribed payment cells | 8,967,692.05 |
| Difference | 0.00 |
| Bank of the United States subscription, retained for reconciliation but excluded analytically | 4,000,000.00 |
| Substantive payments after that exclusion | 4,967,692.05 |

Zeroes were recorded where the printed payment row is blank but the appropriation-purpose column exists. This preserves the complete column structure instead of silently dropping authorized purposes with no reported 1792 payment.

## Debt-service identification

Two payment cells are directly identifiable as public-debt service:

| Purpose | Dollars |
|---|---:|
| Interest on the public debt | 2,374,054.28 |
| Reduction of the public debt | 257,786.42 |
| Identifiable public-debt service | 2,631,840.70 |

The printed heading describes the interest column as domestic debt. The source footnote corrects that description: it covers the entire debt, foreign as well as domestic. The same note warns that the related balance contains two years of foreign-debt interest paid from foreign loans, along with domestic interest not yet funded, registered, or currently payable. Therefore the payment figure is admissible as a use-of-funds observation, but not as proof that customs revenue financed the whole amount.

The table separately reports $435,263.83 paid on the French debt and $18,354.79 paid to foreign officers. Page 44 identifies these as foreign-fund items. They remain in the printed payment control but are flagged so they are not silently attributed to domestic revenue.

## Analytic treatment

- Preserve the $4,000,000 Bank subscription in the reconciliation layer.
- Exclude it from substantive-spending and debt-service denominators because Statement A documents the circular subscription transaction and canceled warrants.
- Treat the $2,631,840.70 public-debt-service total as a purpose-side measure only.
- Do not infer a direct customs-to-debt cash trace from this statement. That requires compatible source-of-funds evidence.
- Keep foreign-fund payments visible and separately flagged.
- Use the printed total only as an arithmetic control, not as a clean measure of economic expenditure.

## Falsification consequence

This statement strengthens the claim that public credit absorbed a large share of recorded federal cash uses in 1792. It does **not** by itself establish customs dominance, causal financing, or the counterfactual importance of customs revenue. P1 still requires the receipts-side federal panel and compatible denominator rules before any dominance ratio is admitted.
