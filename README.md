# America’s Founding Bet

A source-driven historical research and writing project examining whether a geographically dispersed republic could develop enough fiscal, informational, and transportation capacity to survive at continental scale.

## Working thesis

The founding bet was larger than representative government. The United States also had to solve problems of distance, public credit, territorial administration, market access, political coordination, and unequal incorporation.

This is a research hypothesis, not a conclusion assumed in advance.

## Current status

**Archival source collection and claim definition.**

The repository currently contains a first verified source tranche for:

- the Confederation’s institutional limits;
- the Constitution’s national framework;
- territorial organization and admission of new states;
- the geographic scale inherited after independence;
- early federal fiscal administration; and
- the population and territorial baseline around 1790.

The source register records stable public archival links. It does not reproduce entire documents or imply that catalog records establish the project’s historical claims.

## Evidence architecture

```text
archival record
→ normalized source register
→ BIBFRAME-aligned resource metadata
→ historical claim
→ extract or measurement
→ analysis, map, or econometric result
→ Substack interpretation
```

BIBFRAME is used as a bibliographic spine. Archival hierarchy, provenance, and claims remain separate rather than being forced into a single metadata model.

## Source rules

- Prefer stable public records from holding institutions.
- Preserve the holding institution’s identifier and landing page.
- Distinguish original items, digital reproductions, transcripts, and later editions.
- Record archival hierarchy when available.
- Use authority URIs where verified.
- Do not merge records solely because titles or names resemble one another.
- Separate direct evidence from inference and contextual use.
- Record rights and reuse information before reproducing images or substantial text.
